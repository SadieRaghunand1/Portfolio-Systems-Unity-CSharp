Context for game:
The camera in the project can be opened and closed by the player.  When open, this activates a separate Unity camera from the main camera, and projects its feed onto a render texture, which is displayed on a plane in the  player's view.  The purpose of the camera is to be able to capture and score images based on the rarity of the bird captured, the angle and clarity of the subject, and the amount of the subject that is visible in the photo.  These photos are saved between sessions onto the player's computer, are loaded into a journal based on player selection/score assigned, and can be deleted from within the game.

How the system works
The system works on two objects: the in-game camera and the birds.  When the player enters a range within a bird, it prepares to store the data from that bird (name, rarity, etc.) in the event a picture is taken.  When a picture is taken, an automatic 0 is assigned to the score if the player  is not in range of a bird, and if it is in range, the following procedure is executed.

The birds are broken into several game object parts that produce a linecast to the camera to determine whether any objects are blocking the camera's view of the bird, gather how far the bird is from the camera, and calculate how off-center the bird is in the photo.  Based on this and the rarity of the bird, a score is calculated and assigned.  

When saving images, several folders are created on the player's computer to differentiate between subject types, either the type of bird or photos with no birds in them.  Bird photos are saved with their score in the file name, and resaved if the player chooses a specific photo to display in the journal (otherwise the journal defaults to select the photo with the highest score of that bird type).  These photos are loaded into the journal and cached to better optimize the system. From the journal, the player can also select photos to delete, which fully removes the photo from the computer.
